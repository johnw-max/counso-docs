# Configure SSO, directory sync, and group roles

Identity configuration is an administrator task. It connects your identity provider to the workspace, then keeps membership and group changes aligned with the provider. Use one administrator who can work in both systems and keep a second sign-in path available until a test account succeeds.

## Before you begin

Confirm that your workspace administration area exposes SSO or directory-sync controls and that your role can manage them. Prepare an identity-provider administrator, a test account, and a small test group. Do not enable enforcement or deprovisioning for the whole workspace until the test result is clear.

## Configure SAML sign-in

1. Open the administration area and choose the SSO or Single Sign-On setup shown for your workspace.
2. Keep the setup page open. Copy the service values it displays, such as the assertion-consumer URL and entity or audience identifier, into a new SAML application in your identity provider.
3. In the identity provider, map the required identity attributes using its own directory fields and the labels shown by the setup screen. The account email must identify the same person; include the name fields and any verification attribute that the screen requires. Use values generated by your systems rather than examples from a document.
4. Return to the workspace setup and enter the identity-provider sign-in URL and certificate or other values requested by the interface. Save the configuration.
5. Test a dedicated account in a private window. Confirm that it reaches the expected workspace and that its name and email are correct.
6. If the setup offers automatic joining, decide whether it fits your domain policy. Enable enforcement only after the test succeeds; enforcement can sign out people who are still using another sign-in method.

## Provision members and groups

If the administration area exposes directory synchronization, start its setup and select the identity provider presented by the interface. Follow the generated connection instructions in both systems. Provision a test user and test group, then verify that creation, an attribute update, and deactivation are reflected in the workspace before assigning the connection to the full directory.

Use groups to represent durable access decisions. A synchronized group can be mapped to a workspace role when the Roles or Groups control is available. A manually managed workspace group can also be eligible for a mapping. A group grants at most one mapped role, while a person keeps the highest role received across their groups. Removing a person from a synchronized group should be treated as an access change and tested with the same care as adding one.

## What you should see afterward

The test account signs in through the configured identity provider, the test group appears with the expected membership, and the role mapping gives only the intended workspace permissions. Record the group-to-role mapping and the identity-provider application owner so that future membership changes have a clear owner.

## Common questions

### Should I enforce SSO immediately?

No. First verify a test account, a normal member account, and an administrator recovery path. Enforcement can sign out members who have not yet used SAML.

### Can a group grant more than one role?

A group can map to one role. A person can still receive a higher effective role through another group.

### Why is directory sync unavailable?

The feature may be limited to your role or workspace configuration. Ask a workspace administrator to confirm that the control is enabled before changing the identity provider.
